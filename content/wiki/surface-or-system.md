Is an MCQ really that useful of an interface? The heavy lifting is the computation done around it.

I think just MCQs are not the full shape yet, there is probably something else and something better, maybe different things for different shapes. E.g. a slider, a toggle, etc etc. These are effectively logical rules, there is so much overlap here to [[argument-sandbox|the interfaces demo]] (how do we visually distinguish different parts of an idea).

So don't get too caught up in the exact mechanism of a MCQ, it could have the benefit of just reduced noise to the user rather than the means itself being the most useful. E.g. it could be inheriting the benefit of a more robust system with better memory etc.

Why is a list of options the most ideal?

- Limits decision surface area (well the model is already doing a lot of the deciding, that is an assumption and could also be true for other interfaces).

I think I need to be careful here not to conflate a superior predictive system, one that puts immense effort into modeling the user's wants, and the ideal interface.
